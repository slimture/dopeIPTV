# dopeIPTV — Full Feature List

- **Runs on Linux, macOS and Windows** from one codebase — the same embedded libmpv player on all three (Linux is the primary target). Linux: AppImage and `.deb` for x86_64 and ARM64, Flatpak for x86_64; macOS: a `.dmg` for Apple Silicon (macOS 15+) and one for Intel (macOS 14+); Windows: an installer and a portable `.zip`, each for x64 and ARM64. On macOS and Windows, opening a video, a folder or a backup uses the system's own file dialog
- **Your provider**: an **Xtream Codes** login (server, username, password), an **M3U / M3U8** playlist URL, or a **Stalker / Ministra portal** (portal URL + MAC address; *experimental* — channels, films, series, the guide's now/next, and catch-up from the portal's own archive, by programme). Settings are saved; the setup wizard also offers **demo channels** (free public test streams) to try the app without an account
- **Receivers and TV servers at home as sources**: an **Enigma2** receiver (VU+, Dreambox, Zgemma and the like, over OpenWebif or the Dreambox WebInterface) with its bouquets, **Tvheadend** with its channel tags and stream profiles, an **HDHomeRun** tuner or one of its emulators, **VDR** (restfulapi with streamdev-server; streamdev alone gives the channels without a guide), **DVBViewer Media Server**, **NextPVR** (v5 and later, with its PIN) and **MythTV** (its channels in the guide and its recordings — MythTV has no live TV over HTTP, and the app says so). Each comes with its channels, groups and logos, its own guide and its recordings as films, and **Record on the box** in a programme's menu sets a timer on the box itself. Presets in the Add source form fill in a Fritz!Box, Channels DVR, Threadfin, xTeVe, Dispatcharr or ErsatzTV
- **SAT>IP servers** as a source: a Fritz!Box Cable, Digital Devices' Octopus NET, Telestar's Digibit, Kathrein's EXIP, minisatip or Tvheadend's SAT>IP server — found with **Find on my network** (also on the other parts of a network split in VLANs), its tuners read from the server, its channels from its own list (a Fritz!Box's HD, SD and radio lists as groups) or from an M3U you give it (a file or a web address, as lists are published for each satellite and cable network). Channels keep their favourites when a list is reordered; they play over **RTSP over TCP** (fine on Wi-Fi), or UDP or HTTP where the server wants it. Each channel takes one of the server's tuners, and before a multiview window, a recording or a cast would need one more than it has, the app asks first. SAT>IP sends no guide: give the source an XMLTV address and the guide works as for any other
- **Media servers — Jellyfin, Emby and Plex**: sign in once (Jellyfin's and Emby's name and password are sent once and never kept, Jellyfin also takes Quick Connect, Plex signs in with a PIN in the browser and you pick the server), then films by library, series with their seasons and episodes, recordings, and Live TV with the server's guide where the server has it (Plex: with its DVR, which needs a tuner and Plex Pass on the owner's side — the app says so). The server's artwork, resume points and watched marks show on the rows; the newer of the server's and this computer's resume point is offered, the server's partly watched titles join Continue watching, what you play is reported back to the server and a watched mark made here goes back too — nothing during private viewing. A source plays the file itself, or (Nerd) the server's own HLS at a bitrate
- **Find on my network**: the Add source form looks for boxes and media servers by itself — the HDHomeRun, Jellyfin/Emby and Plex broadcasts, and every address of the local network on the ports these servers use — and pressed again it stops. With nothing found it says why a home network split in VLANs hides a box and what fixes it; a box can always be added by its address, on any part of the network
- **A page for each source** under Settings → Sources: its connection, its guide address and auto-refresh, its stream format, and while Nerd is on its network and stream options and a Test connection — each row marked as this source's own or shared, and where the shared one lives. Adding a source keeps the quick form (presets, Find on my network, a media server's sign-in) and then opens the new source's page
- **An Enigma2 box from the app** (a channel's right-click → the box's menu): zap the box, wake it or put it in standby, its info with the live signal (SNR, AGC, BER — also in Stats for nerds while they show), a screenshot of its TV (picture, menus or both) with Save, and its remote control. Its recordings are renamed, moved and deleted from a recording's menu, and a channel the box cannot decrypt is said so plainly — in the player, the stream health and recordings — instead of a black picture. With Nerd, a source can play the box's transcoded stream (port 8002, Low to Full HD)
- **The box's tuners counted**: channels on one transponder share a tuner, so the app asks an Enigma2 box what its tuners are doing — its recordings and what its own TV shows — and asks first, with Go ahead anyway, before a zap from any way in, a multiview cell, a cast or a recording would cut one of them off. A quiet ring in the channel list marks the channels that would. A box that does not answer never holds playback up
- **AutoTimer on the box**: on an Enigma2 box with the AutoTimer plugin, Settings → Recording → Series recordings lists the box's own rules after the app's, each row saying who records it — new, edit, turn on or off, preview what it would record, Run now. "Record series…" on a box's programme offers the rule on the box (words and how they match, channels from the box's bouquets, time span, days, padding, record or zap, folder, duplicates). What the box records itself, the app leaves to it
- TV, Movies, and Series with seasons and episodes, and Mine with your Favorites, Watch later and History
- **A page for every series**: opening a series - from its list, Mine, Continue watching, New episodes, the command palette or an actor's titles - shows its own page where the list is: the backdrop (or poster), title, year, rating, genres and plot, one button that plays on (**Continue S2 E5**, with how far you got, or **Play S1 E1**), Favorite, Follow and Watch later, the **seasons in one row** (Specials last; the one you picked is remembered) and the episodes as cards with their picture, length, plot, a watched check and a progress bar, the next one to watch highlighted. An episode plays in the docked player and the page stays, following autoplay to the next episode; Back (the arrow, Esc or the mouse's back button) returns to the list exactly where it was. Arrow keys move through seasons and episodes, Enter plays, right-click has the episode's menu. Works for Xtream, Stalker portals, Jellyfin, Emby, Plex and your own files' library; TV mode's series screen has the same season row
- **27 languages**: the whole interface is translated into English, Svenska, Español, Deutsch, Français, 中文, Русский, ไทย, Português, Italiano, Nederlands, Polski, Hrvatski, Srpski, Ελληνικά, Türkçe, Українська, Bahasa Indonesia, Tiếng Việt, हिन्दी, 日本語, 한국어, Kiswahili, العربية, فارسی, עברית and اردو — pick your language under Settings → General and the menus switch **live**, no restart. The four right-to-left languages (Arabic, Persian, Hebrew, Urdu) mirror the layout automatically. English is the inline source; every other language ships as a reviewable `dopeiptv/locale/<code>.json` file
- Categories in the sidebar and fast, debounced search — no cap on the number of channels, and no freeze even with thousands of them (the channel list is virtualized, so only visible rows are ever rendered)
- **The sidebar is the app**: Mine, TV, Movies, Series, Guide and Search at the top, Local files, Radio and Recordings under a hairline, with a quiet status beside them: a red dot while something records, new episodes, the watch-later count; the source in use at its foot. Every place has the same calm header - its name, how many it holds and in which order, the sort and its switch - and Back, Esc or the mouse's back button return to the place you came from exactly as you left it. The app opens where you left off (each profile its own place) or always on Mine (Settings → General → Start)
- **Room for the list** (Settings → Appearance → What the places show, each profile its own): the places fold into a compact block of icons at the sidebar's top (the small arrow under them), giving a long list of categories its room, with the entry you are on still lit and its marks on its icon; Guide, Search, Local files, Radio and Recordings can each be taken out of the sidebar (they still open from their shortcuts, the command palette and the menus); a right click on a row's heading - Recently watched, Continue, Popular right now, Recording now and next, My stations - hides it, and Settings brings it back; **Just the list** shows no rows over TV, Movies, Series and Local files, smaller headers with the count beside the name, Movies and Series as rows and TV compact, and gives everything back as it was once it is off
- **Mine**: what you were watching (films, episodes and local files, from any source), your favourite channels live now in the order you usually watch at this hour and weekday ("Usually watched now"), your favourite films and series, Watch later as a queue you order yourself (a title you watched leaves it by itself), new episodes of the series you follow and History as a timeline by day with Play, Continue or Play again; one search and a row of chips filter all of it, and your favourite folders are a tab of their own, dragged into order
- **TV, Movies and Series as places**: TV shows the category's name and count, Recently watched as small cards and the channels as rows with what is on, how far it got and what comes next (Compact or Airy); Movies and Series show posters or a list with **Popular right now** - this week's chart from TMDB matched to your own source, switched off in Settings → Posters & info - and **Continue** (a series' next episode, "S2 E5 · 18 min left"); a click on a film shows its plot, cast and a **Continue** button with how far you got in the column beside the list, and a double-click or Enter plays it
- EPG shown immediately for every visible live channel (not just the one you click), using a "now playing" line and progress bar
- **One search across every source** (Search in the sidebar, Ctrl+Shift+F, the guide's Search button, or "Search everywhere for …" in the command palette): type "Star Trek" or "Vegas" and the Search page lists what every source has - channels, programmes in the guide from now to a week ahead (title and description), movies and series - in four groups with their counts, a few of each and **Show all**, each row with its logo or poster and a line naming its source ("SVT1 HD · Pirat · Today 21:00–22:30"); a programme on now has a Live mark and how far it has got. Enter or a double-click plays a channel or what is on now, sets a reminder for a programme later (its menu: Remind me, Record), plays a movie with the usual resume question or opens a series' page - and the results stay, so you can try another; Back from a series comes to them as they were. A channel of another source plays without switching to it, the status line saying where it is from; its reminders fire whichever source is in use. A movie or series of another source switches to that source first, the results kept. Hidden, locked and (for a kids profile) adult categories are never searched, a portal is searched only as far as its lists were opened (the page says so), no guide is downloaded for it and nothing opens a stream until you play a hit
- **The guide** in the middle of the window (Guide in the sidebar) or in a window of its own (Pop out): a timeline grid of every channel with a filter, following the category you pick in the sidebar while it is open over TV (a double click on a category opens it there), its hours fitted to the room it has or picked with − / + (Ctrl + wheel). A programme on now has a strip of how far it has got; **Settings → Guide → Line at the time now** (off by default) draws a line through the whole guide at the current time, and the channel column can be dragged wider or narrower
- EPG fallback chain: short EPG → full EPG table (`get_simple_data_table`) → the provider's XMLTV guide (`xmltv.php`), matched by EPG channel id or channel name — listings are still shown when the server's timestamps look skewed, and duplicate entries from buggy providers are filtered out
- Refresh the EPG with one click — it also refreshes automatically when the current program ends, and the whole guide is looked at every 20 minutes and when you come back to the app, and fetched again when it is old or about to run out (so a guide left open overnight does not stay on yesterday). A ↻ button in the guide window refreshes it too, and the detail panel says plainly when a provider's guide for a channel ends before now
- **Your own files, in the same app**: point **Local files** at a folder — or mount a network share and it appears by itself — and browse what is on your own disks beside what your provider sends. A place like the others: the folder's name with how many folders and files it holds, a way back to the folder above, the filter, Rescan and two views in its header — **folders**, which shows the tree as it is on disk, its files with their pictures, size and length, and **library**, which reads the file names and builds series with seasons and episodes out of them, as posters or rows — with **Continue** at the top for what you stopped in the middle of. It **notices changes by itself**: a file renamed, added or removed shows a moment later without Rescan, the list staying where you were (a network share, which sends no word of what another computer did, is read again lightly every few minutes while you look). Films get **TMDB posters and details** like anything else, episodes and films **resume where you stopped**, watching is written to **History** (its own "Local" category) and **scrobbled to Trakt** when the title was matched. Local files **cast to a Chromecast** and can be opened in **multiview** like a channel. If a file has moved or the share is not mounted, History says so plainly and offers to drop the entry rather than failing to play
- **Music too**: audio files get their album art (read from the tags, and looked up online when a folder has none), and a **play queue** — add a track, add everything in a folder, or play something next, and it moves on to the next track by itself when one ends. Settings → Local files holds the folder list; the library, poster and thumbnail caches are cleared under Settings → Advanced → Caches. **F5** (rebindable) rescans what Local files shows
- **Internet radio** (Radio, under Local files): radio-browser.info's directory — My stations, most voted, most listened, a search, and the countries (with their flags), languages and genres — as a place like the others: the list's name with how many stations it holds, the filter (the whole directory's search under Search) and **Grid / List** in its header, **My stations** as a row over every list of stations, then the stations as rows or tiles with their logos, where each is from and what it plays, a star on your favourites and what the station playing says is on. A station plays as music, with the visualiser, the equaliser and its current title, and is kept in History; favourite stations sit beside your other favorites, in backups and in sync. A click is reported to the directory only when you turn that on (Settings → Integrations), never during private viewing; requests carry only "dopeIPTV/<version>"
- **YouTube Live, Twitch and similar sites** (File → Open address…, `Ctrl`+`L`, or `--play` and `dopeiptv://` links): played through yt-dlp — the system's own, or the official release the app downloads into your data folder when you ask, checks against its published checksums and keeps up to date (Settings → Playback → YouTube, Twitch and similar sites). It is never in our packages. Saved addresses become favorites with a LIVE badge, and web pages play with mpv's own network defaults rather than the ones tuned for IPTV
- **TV mode** (View → TV mode, `Ctrl`+`Shift`+`T`, `dopeiptv --tv`, or Start in TV mode under Settings → General): a full-screen layout for the sofa — Home (continue watching, favorites, live now), Channels (categories, then channels with now and next), the guide in large, Movies and Series in posters with a series' episodes, Search with a keyboard on screen, and Options (audio track, subtitles, Record - with a REC mark over the picture while it records -, sleep timer, start in TV mode, leave). Large type inside safe margins, the theme's colours with the accent as the focus ring. Everything works with the arrows, OK, Back, Menu, Play/Pause, channel up/down, digits, volume and the colour keys (red: favorite, green: guide, yellow: search, blue: options). A remote that acts as a keyboard and an HDMI-CEC remote the system turns into keys always work; a game controller (through SDL2 when pysdl2 is installed, else Linux's own input devices) and LIRC remotes too. While something plays, a Now playing row above the tabs brings the picture back, and Back over the picture returns to the page with the stream going on. Leaving is always one step away: Back on Home asks once and leaves on the next press, and Options → Leave TV mode, `Ctrl`+`Shift`+`T`, the View menu (checked while TV mode is on) and the picture's own exit-fullscreen button all end it - the window comes back exactly as it was, the docked player and its panes included
- **Command palette** (`Ctrl`+`K`, `⌘`+`K` on a Mac, rebindable): one field over the window finds the menu's entries and the shortcuts' actions with their keys, any setting (Settings opens on its page with the row lit), the active source's channels by name or number, and the guide's programmes from now on — a channel plays, a programme offers play, record and remind. Case, accents and punctuation do not matter, and it stays quick over tens of thousands of channels. What you searched for is kept only until the app closes, and not at all during private viewing

- **Rich movie & series details**: plot, genre, cast, director, release date and rating in the detail panel, with a large poster. Artwork, rating (linked to IMDb), release date and a clickable cast list come from **TMDB** — built in, no key needed (or use your own free key) — and **filled in from TMDB even for titles your provider returns no info for**
- **Explore the cast**: click an actor's name (or photo) to open a panel of their other movies/series found in your playlist, each with a poster thumbnail (double-click to open it). The panel stays on top without blocking the app, so you can keep clicking other cast members while it's open
- **Resume where you left off**: movies, series episodes and recordings remember your playback position; starting one again asks whether to **resume at that time or start from the beginning** (positions near the start/end are ignored)
- Favorites: save channels into your own groups, browse them in Mine, remove channels or whole groups via right-click
- **Profiles, one per person in the household** (Settings → Profiles, up to eight): each has its own favorites, history and resume points, Watch later, watched marks, followed series, reminders, statistics, Trakt and Simkl, where the app opens, theme and accent, and audio and subtitle languages; sources, recordings, the guide, devices, Nerd and the parental lock are the household's. "Who's watching?" at start (big tiles, the keys, a screen of its own in TV mode), an avatar in a colour or an emoji, a four-digit PIN if you like; switching from the avatar's menu stops what plays and shows the profile's own, with no restart. A **kids profile** never sees a locked or adult category and opens Settings only with a grown-up's PIN. The phone remote, the remote API and Home Assistant show and switch the profile; sync and the full backup carry every profile, and sync never carries a PIN
- **Your statistics** (History → Your statistics…): time watched this week, month and year, by day of the week and by hour, the channels, movies and series you watch most and your longest streak - counted on this computer only and never sent anywhere; private viewing adds nothing, and Clear recent history clears it too
- **Private viewing** (`Ctrl`+`Shift`+`N`): nothing of what you watch is kept until the app closes — no history, resume points, watched marks, statistics or scrobbles, and titles stay out of the log — and a mark in the sidebar says it is on. A playback that started private stays private to its end. **Clear recent history** (`Ctrl`+`Shift`+`Del`) clears the last hour, 3 hours, 24 hours, 5 days or all of it, like a browser's
- History: every played channel/movie/episode is recorded with a resolved playback URL and its poster/metadata; browse, replay, remove entries (with full **multi-select**: Ctrl/Shift-click, Ctrl+A, rubber-band drag, then right-click → remove or press Delete), or clear everything
- **What the app is doing, at the foot of the sidebar**: a quiet activity line shows the work under way - a list loading, the guide downloading with its progress, a source switched - and what the app has to tell you waits there as messages, with anything that needs a decision on a small card above it; nothing covers the list or the video
- **Embedded in-app video**: with `python-mpv` + libmpv installed, channels play directly inside the app's detail panel, rendered via libmpv's OpenGL render API — no window embedding involved, so it works the same regardless of desktop/compositor (GNOME, KDE, Hyprland, ...) and is the default whenever available. Double-click the video or press `F` for fullscreen, `Esc` to leave it. Selecting a new channel switches the stream in place — perfect for zapping through favorites
- **Player controls**: crisp, hand-drawn control icons; pause/resume, stop, previous/next, and a **volume slider + mute** in the player bar and the fullscreen floating controls; volume is remembered between sessions
- **You see that it is loading**: a channel, film or station that takes a moment shows a calm spinner over the picture with its logo or poster (when the app has it already), its name and *Connecting…* — *Still loading* after a while, *Reconnecting…* when the app reconnects a dropped channel, and *Buffering… 45%* when a stream stops to fill its buffer or a seek takes more than a second. A channel that starts at once never flashes it; it is in the docked player, full screen, TV mode, the pop-out window and each multiview window, and clicks go straight through it
- **Rewind live TV**: move the pointer over a live channel and a bar takes you back in it; **LIVE** brings you back, pause and the arrow keys work too. **Settings → Playback → Rewind live channels** sets how far back (2 minutes by default, in memory); **Keep the rewind buffer on disk** goes back up to 6 hours without using memory. Paused past the buffer, the button reads **↻ LIVE** and opens the channel again at live
- **dopeIPTV's own buffer** (*experimental*, **Settings → Playback → Rewind with dopeIPTV's own buffer**): the app keeps the channel itself, on disk, with one provider connection — it goes on downloading while you pause, **LIVE** never reconnects, and you go back as far as set, within the space you give it (the oldest part goes first when it is full or the disk runs low). Each channel you leave keeps its history for the session, and **Record** records from the buffer: whole even while you pause, and from where you are when you have gone back. Without it, an in-player recording stands still while you pause, and the app says so
- On a channel with the provider's catch-up, **DVR | TIMESHIFT** at the left of the bar switches between the rewind buffer and the archive timeline, and the app remembers which you like
- **Seek any way you like** for movies, series and catch-up content: a **hover scrubber** slides up over the bottom of the video (timeline + elapsed/total time + −10s / +30s), the **arrow keys** seek once the video is focused (tap ← / → to jump ±10s, hold to seek continuously), and the fullscreen overlay has its own scrubber. Live streams (which aren't seekable) show no scrubber
- **Pop-out player**: detach the video into its own window (e.g. onto a second screen) — frameless by default, drag anywhere on the picture to move it, right-click for always-on-top and a show/hide title bar toggle, controls auto-hide, `Esc` or right-click → Exit brings it home
- **Multiview**: watch **2, 4, 6 or 9 live channels at once** in its own grid window (size under Settings → Multiview). Each window is an independent stream — mix channels from **different playlists/accounts**, click a window for audio focus (red border marks the audible one), right-click for mute, audio/subtitle track choice, move/swap between windows, or remove. Timeshift channels get a **real archive timeline** (programme ticks from the EPG, hover names the programme, a LIVE pill jumps back to the live edge), each window shows its channel, current programme and source playlist, overlays/cursor auto-hide, double-click maximizes, and multiview viewing is recorded in History. A one-time notice explains the provider-connection cost, and starting playback in the main window offers to close multiview (configurable)
- **Plain-language stream diagnosis**: when a stream won't start, the app probes the provider and tells you *why* in your language — account expired, connection limit reached (458), stream missing, server unreachable, ... — instead of a bare "loading failed"
- **In-player options menu** (the ⚙ button): pick the audio track (language) and subtitle track, adjust audio delay, force an aspect ratio (16:9, 4:3, stretch, ...), set the network buffer/cache size, and view **Stats for nerds** (video/audio codec, resolution, fps, hardware decoding, A/V sync, dropped frames, cache and network speed) — applied live and remembered
- **Permanent playback defaults** under Settings → Playback and Picture & sound: preferred audio language, subtitles off/on (+ a first and second preferred subtitle language), **Subtitles on live TV** (on by default; off, live channels start without subtitles while films and series still get them), default aspect ratio, and network buffer — applied to every stream automatically; the ⚙ menu still overrides per session
- **Stays awake**: while video plays (mini player or fullscreen) the app inhibits the screensaver/suspend via DBus (GNOME/KDE), and releases it on stop/error/exit
- Changing the aspect ratio letterboxes **inside** the player: the mini player's video is pinned to a **constant height** — dragging the splitter, resizing the window, long titles or EPG cards never change its size
- **Remembers your layout**: the sidebar / list / detail panel divider positions are saved on exit and restored next time
- **Themes**: 25 themes picked from small pictures of the app under Settings → Appearance — Graphite, Midnight, OLED pure black, Nord, Dracula, Gruvbox, Solarized, Catppuccin Mocha, Tokyo Night, Rosé Pine, One Dark, Everforest, Kanagawa and High contrast; eight that differ by colour rather than shade — Ocean, Forest, Bordeaux, Synthwave, Amber terminal and Espresso, and the light Sand and Sky; and the light Light, Latte and Solarized light. The accent is a row of ten colours, the theme's own, or any colour you like; the app can follow the desktop's light and dark mode, corners can be sharp or extra rounded, and text and controls larger or smaller — all applied live, no restart
- **Night mode for the sound**: one switch that brings loud adverts, bangs and scenes down towards the level of speech - never louder, and plain speech is left as it is - from the ⚙ menu, the `N` key (rebindable under Settings → Keyboard shortcuts) or Settings → Playback. It turns on at once while the stream plays on, stays on through channel changes, the rewind buffer and the pop-out, and is remembered
- **Stream information** (Settings → Playback, off by default): resolution, frame rate and codec for a moment when you change channels and under the programme's progress bar, and in the channel list the last values seen while it played — no extra stream is opened for it
- **Subtitles your way**: size, colour and a dark box behind them, under Settings → Picture & sound or right-click the video → Subtitles; turning subtitles on in a film is instant (a different language takes a moment)
- **DVB subtitles and teletext subtitles**: a channel's DVB subtitles and teletext subtitle pages are in the player's subtitle menu like any other track, named by language and page ("Swedish - teletext 199"); the page picked is remembered per channel, and your preferred subtitle language picks a teletext page on a live channel. Teletext subtitles need an mpv whose FFmpeg has libzvbi — without it the entries say so, and DVB subtitles still work
- **Teletext** (View → Teletext…, the player's ⚙ menu, or `T` while a live channel plays): pages 100–899 with their subpages, P+/P−, three digits typed anywhere, the Fastext colour keys, index, hold and reveal, drawn in the teletext palette with mosaics and double height and scaled to the window. It is read from dopeIPTV's own rewind buffer for the channel (Settings → Playback → Rewind with dopeIPTV's own buffer), so it never opens a second connection
- **A taller video**: drag the small handle under the docked video; the height is remembered
- Fullscreen niceties: an **"Exit fullscreen" button** right in the floating controls (no need to know about `Esc`), the mouse cursor auto-hides with the controls, and leaving fullscreen returns the channel list to the playing channel instead of jumping to the top
- The window title shows what's currently playing, and the **playing item is highlighted** in every list — TV, Movies, series episodes, Favorites, Recordings and History, in both list and grid view (accent bar/border + colored name)
- **Back / Next** buttons in the player bar zap not only through TV channels but also through Movies, a series' episodes, Recordings and History entries
- **Recording**: right-click a TV channel → Record (or use the **REC button right in the player**) to record the stream locally — **until you stop it**, for a fixed length (30 min / 1 h / 2 h / 4 h), or **scheduled with start and stop timers**. Saved where you choose (Settings → Recording); scheduled jobs survive an app restart (the app must be running when they fire)
- **Recording the channel you're watching uses the same stream** — it taps the embedded player's own connection (mpv stream-record) instead of opening a second one, so it works on the common one-stream-at-a-time IPTV accounts. Recording a *different* channel, or a scheduled job firing later, records over its own connection (ffmpeg, mpv fallback). Note: the file of an in-player recording is finalized when the recording stops — stopping playback, zapping away, or a stream error ends it
- A persistent red **● REC indicator** under the channel list shows whenever something is recording — click it to stop a recording or jump to the Recordings section. The player's REC button also turns into ● REC and its menu has **stop actions** for every active recording
- **Stream-switch protection**: starting another stream while something records pops a clear, compact choice dialog
- **Recording size cap**: optionally stop any recording once its file reaches a limit you set (number + MB/GB/TB) under Settings → Recording — or set a quick **per-session cap right in the player's REC menu** (250 MB … 10 GB / no limit)
- **Recordings as MP4** (Settings → Recording, off by default): once a recording has ended well it is copied into an MP4 that plays anywhere - nothing re-encoded, the index at the front - and the .ts goes only when the MP4 is whole; anything amiss keeps the .ts. The name template applies, and the command after a recording gets the MP4. With **Keep teletext and subtitles**, a recording that has them stays a .ts, since an MP4 cannot carry them
- **Recordings** in the sidebar, a place like the others: the folder's name with how many recordings and what they take on the disk, the filter, ⋯ (scheduled recordings, series recordings, a new folder, the folder itself, another recordings folder) and **Grid / List** in its header; **Recording now and next** at the top — what records, with its red mark and how long it has run, then what is to come, soonest first, each with its stop, edit and cancel in its menu — then the recordings as rows or 16:9 cards with **a frame of each** (else its channel's logo), its channel, day, length and size, how far you watched it and **Watched** once played to the end (or marked so from its menu; each profile its own). Subfolders are categories, create folders, rename, move between folders, multi-select delete (right-click or the Delete key), open a recording's folder — and watch a recording **while it's still being recorded**. "Active & scheduled" shows running/pending jobs; select several and remove them at once, "Clear all finished from list" wipes the history, and deleting a recording file removes its job entry too
- **Series recording**: "Record series…" on a programme in the guide records every airing the guide shows for the week ahead — by exact title or a word, on one channel or any — never twice, a cancelled one not again, a programme on two channels at once only once, and a few minutes before and after if you like (Settings → Recording → Padding). The same window can **remind** instead (and the guide has "Remind me of every airing…"): a reminder at each airing's start, never twice, and one you removed is not set again. A reminder from the guide keeps its programme's end
- **Export to calendar** (File → Export to calendar…, and in the Reminders window): your reminders and scheduled recordings as an .ics file — each programme an event at its time, a reminder with an alert when it starts — or as a calendar to subscribe to while the remote API or the phone remote runs
- **Timeshift / catch-up detection**: channels whose provider keeps an archive (`tv_archive`) get a ⏪ marker in the list and a "Catch-up: N days" note in the detail panel; right-click → Timeshift — or the **⏪ button right in the player** (windowed and fullscreen) — to **watch the current programme from the start**, **browse past programmes from the EPG** (grouped by day, double-click to watch), or jump back in steps that **scale dynamically with the provider's actual archive depth** (30 min up to 7 days). The archive chunk is seekable like a movie
- **Edit your channel list**: right-click any channel/movie/series to rename or hide it (persisted per playlist — effectively your own edited playlist), and "Restore default channels..." undoes it all back to exactly what the provider sends
- **An Enigma2 box's own channel lists**: on a VU+, Dreambox, Zgemma or any box with OpenWebif, right-click a channel → The box → "Edit the box's channel lists…" edits its bouquets from the computer, as Dreamboxedit does - TV and radio bouquets made, renamed, moved and deleted, channels dragged into order (or Ctrl+↑ / Ctrl+↓), renamed and removed, markers and spacers, and channels added from everything the box knows, by satellite or provider, with a search. Each change goes straight to the box, in the order made; the box first takes a backup of every list, which you can save and put back with Undo, and the app reads the new order in once the window closes. Never touches what is playing
- **Persistent EPG cache**: the XMLTV guide is saved to disk per playlist, so after a restart the EPG shows immediately from the previous session (a cache younger than 6 hours skips the download entirely). When the cache is older, **last session's guide is shown instantly** while the fresh download replaces it in the background — and if the download fails, the stale data stays
- **EPG refresh with progress**: "Refresh EPG" force re-downloads the guide with a real progress bar (percent when the server reports a size, animated otherwise) and then updates both the panel and the now-playing lines in the channel list
- **Multiple sources**: save several providers, accounts, boxes and servers, switch between them at runtime (the button with the source's initials at the foot of the sidebar), drag them into the order you want, back them all up to a file and import them elsewhere, or drop an M3U file or link on the window to add one (favorites and history are kept per playlist), edit them, give each an optional **custom TV guide (XMLTV) URL** that overrides the provider's own, and pick an **auto-refresh** cadence per playlist (never / at startup / every 2, 6, 12 hours / daily / weekly)
- **Content manager**: right-click any category (or "Manage categories...") to hide it, rename it, or lock it — hidden/locked categories' channels are also left out of "All"
- **Parental control**: a salted+hashed PIN protects locked categories and locked favorite groups; locked favorite groups don't reveal which channels they contain until the PIN is entered
- **Connections to the account**: the account details say how many of its connections are in use ("1 of 2"), and the app counts its own — the player, rewind buffers, recordings over their own connection, multiview cells and a cast. Before a multiview cell, a recording while you watch, the rewind buffer beside a recording or a cast would go over the account's limit, it says so plainly, with Go ahead anyway
- **Chromecast**: right-click any channel/movie/episode → "Cast to TV..." to scan the network and cast to a device (live streams are cast as HLS; a sound track a Chromecast cannot play, such as E-AC-3, is converted on the way). pychromecast is inside every packaged build, and `pip`/`pipx` installs it from source
- **Smart TVs (DLNA / UPnP)**: the same "Cast to TV..." panel finds Samsung, LG, Sony, Philips and Panasonic TVs, AV receivers and apps like Kodi, listed as "Name (DLNA)". The TV fetches the stream itself; a local file is served to it from the app with byte ranges, so the TV can seek. Pause, seek, volume and mute from the cast card. Nothing to install
- **Kodi**: the same panel lists Kodi boxes found on the network, as "Name (Kodi)", and "Add Kodi..." takes one by its address with its web server's user name and password (Kodi: Settings → Services → Control → Allow remote control via HTTP). Kodi is handed the provider's own address with the source's user agent and headers, and the cast card shows where it has got to: pause, seek in a film, volume, mute - and the cast ends when Kodi is stopped with its own remote
- **The cast card**: while a cast runs, a card stands in the video's place - the poster or logo, the title and what is on or which episode, the TV it plays on, and its state (Starting, Playing, Paused, Buffering…, Reconnecting…), with play/pause, 30 s back and forward (on a channel only where it can go back), Stop, the timeline with LIVE, the volume and the tracks. A TV stuck buffering for 10 seconds is sent the stream again by itself, twice, and then the card offers **Try again** (a stream that has not started yet gets 45 seconds); it never tries by itself where the account's connections are all taken. What plays on the TV is marked **On TV** in every list
- **A server for the home** (Settings → Nerd → Remote and automation, off by default, or `dopeiptv --server` without a window - for a NAS or a Raspberry Pi): VLC, Kodi and a TV's IPTV app watch your sources through dopeIPTV from its playlist, and **Plex, Jellyfin and Emby find it by themselves as an HDHomeRun tuner**, with the guide under the tuner's channel numbers - kept fresh by the server itself, each source's downloaded again when it is six hours old. Each channel is fetched once and shared by everyone watching it - the app's own player too - and never more channels at once than your account allows: your own viewing and recordings always come first, and a device that would need one more is told all tuners are in use. Home network only; a page in the browser shows who watches what and holds its few settings (port, channels at once, which sources and categories - never a hidden or locked one -, and whether channels for adults go out too: off unless you turn it on)
- **AirPlay to an Apple TV** (macOS): "AirPlay (Apple TV)" in the Cast to TV panel opens a small window with Apple's own AirPlay button; pick the Apple TV there and it fetches the stream itself — the Mac plays nothing. HLS and MP4 an Apple TV decodes go as they are; anything else is repackaged by the bundled ffmpeg as HLS on a small server on the home network, under a random address (H.264/HEVC and AAC/AC-3/E-AC-3 copied, other sound to AAC and MPEG-2 pictures to H.264, which the panel says costs processor time). The cast card pauses and stops it, seeks in a film or an episode and brings the AirPlay window back; a live channel plays live on the Apple TV - going back in it is not offered there, since each step back was a new stream the Apple TV started over, slow and out of step with its sound - and the Apple TV's own remote sets the volume. A macOS firewall that blocks the app is said, with how to allow it
- **Casting goes on**: a live channel picked while a cast runs — from the list, the guide, a typed number, the last-channel key, the phone remote, Home Assistant or the media keys — goes to the TV that is casting instead of the player, and so does a film or an episode, with its resume point and its sound track and subtitles where the TV takes them; at an episode's end the next one follows on the TV. Only Stop casting ends a cast. A running cast counts as one of the account's connections, and casting asks first when one more would go over the limit
- **Going back while casting**: the cast card's timeshift button and timeline go back in the provider's archive, and to a Chromecast in the app's own rewind buffer for as long as it holds the moment; the archive and the buffer reach the TV as one growing playlist, so it plays on where a piece ended and a move within what is written is a seek on the TV. By default the buffer is kept only while the cast reads from it; **Settings → Casting → Keep the rewind buffer while casting** (off by default) keeps it recording beside a live cast, which takes one more connection to the provider — over a full or unknown limit the app asks first. A DLNA TV or a Kodi, which fetch the stream themselves, goes back in the archive only; an Apple TV plays the channel live
- **Devices on another part of the network**: "Add a device by address…" in the Cast to TV panel takes a Chromecast, a DLNA TV or a Kodi by its address (the kind found out, or chosen); it is kept, listed after a restart beside what the searches find, and removed there or under Settings → Casting. With Nerd, the panel also asks the other networks listed under Nerd → Network → Other networks
- **Subtitles from OpenSubtitles.com**: the player's Subtitles menu → "Find subtitles online..." searches for the film, episode or local file that is playing (by TMDB id, by series + season + episode, or by the file's hash) in your subtitle language, ranks the hits (exact file match first, then human-made, trusted and popular) and loads the one you pick. A subtitle loaded for a title comes back with it the next time it plays. Release builds can carry an API key; without one, a free key from opensubtitles.com goes under Settings → Integrations. An account is optional: without one OpenSubtitles allows 5 downloads a day per internet connection, with a free account 20, and the search says so when the day's downloads are used up. "Load subtitle file..." loads one from disk
- **System media controls**: on Linux (MPRIS) GNOME's and KDE's media controls, the keyboard's media keys, headphone buttons and KDE Connect on a phone; on macOS Now Playing in Control Center, on the lock screen and in the menu bar, the keyboard's media keys and AirPods; on Windows the media controls by the volume, on the lock screen and the keyboard's media keys. They pause, play, skip channels or episodes and seek, and show what is playing - the channel and its programme, or the title and its position - with its logo. The media keys also work inside the window. Can be turned off under Settings → General
- **Popular right now**: at the top of Movies and of Series, TMDB's chart of the week - only the titles your provider has, with their chart position, a category's own in a category; switched on or off for each under Settings → Posters & info
- **Follow a series**: right-click a series → "Follow series". A few minutes after start and then every few hours the provider is asked what each followed series lists; new episodes appear in Mine under **New episodes**, in a note at the foot of the sidebar and — on Linux, with the window in the background — as a desktop notification. Opening the series opens its page on the first new episode and marks the new ones. Settings → Mine → **Followed series** turns the announcements off (Mine's section and the dots stay) and lists the followed series, each with Stop following
- **Trakt** (optional): scrobble what you watch and browse your watchlist/history — Settings → Watch history → Connect via browser, one click and no keys to create
- **Simkl** (optional): the same with a Simkl account, beside Trakt or instead of it — Settings → Watch history → Connect with a code. Scrobbles, watched marks, Watch later and the watched badges go to (and come from) every account signed in
- **Discord status** (optional): "Show what I watch in Discord" under Settings → Integrations puts "Watching <channel>", the film or the series and episode, with the time it has been on, in your Discord status - or only "Watching TV". Nothing during private viewing
- **Phone remote** (View → Phone remote…, off until you turn it on): scan the QR code with the phone's camera and a page opens in its browser — the current source's channels with search, favorites and logos, play, channel up and down, play/pause, stop, volume and mute, and what is on now and next. Beside Channels and Favorites it browses **Movies, Series** (a series' Episodes view, by season, with what you have watched), **Recent** (Continue watching and History), **Local files, Radio and Recordings**, and plays what you pick where it got to; a film or an episode is sought on a bar, a live channel in the rewind buffer is wound back on the same bar with **Live** to return; **Record** records what plays (asked first) or the next programme, and stops a recording; a sheet picks the **sound track and subtitles**, another sets the **sleep timer**. What the app would ask on the computer - a connection limit, a recording in the way - is asked on the phone, and nothing starts without an answer. While TV mode is on the page becomes a remote: a d-pad with OK, Back and Options, the colour keys, volume and channel rockers, mute and number keys, or a touchpad (a swipe for the arrows, a tap for OK, a long press for Back). Added to the phone's home screen it opens as **dopeIPTV Remote** with the app's icon. Only devices on the home network reach it, behind a long random address, and **New address** shuts out every phone that had the old one. Nothing to install on the phone
- **Watch together** (View → Watch together…): start a room and share its code (ten characters without look-alikes, like K7PQM-2XDH9); friends join with it and watch the same film or episode in step, each from their own source — matched by TMDB id, else title and year — with play, pause and seek mirrored and positions kept within about a second. A live channel is tuned on each person's own source, and the strip under the video says who is in and why a channel cannot be in exact step. A title someone's source lacks is said plainly; the others go on. The room id and the key every message is sealed with both come from the code, so dopeiptv.org only passes encrypted messages on and never sees names or what plays. Not during private viewing
- **Sync between computers** (Settings → Sync, for everyone): favorites with their folders and settings — and, if you like, playlists and watch history — the same on every computer, for every profile. Encrypted on the computer with a key made from a sync code only your computers know; dopeiptv.org keeps only encrypted bytes under a random number, with no account, e-mail or name, and deletes a copy unused for 180 days. It cannot change a copy unnoticed, and an earlier copy handed back is told: every copy carries a sealed counter, and a computer that has seen a later one says so and puts its own back. The newest change wins per entry and a removal travels too; a sync never runs in a stream's first seconds
- **Back up everything** (Settings → General → Backup): settings, sources with their logins, every profile's favorites and history, planned recordings and Nerd in one file, shown before it is restored on another computer. **An optional password** encrypts the file (ChaCha20-Poly1305, the key from the password by PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 in 600 000 rounds, all on this computer) and is asked for when it is restored; without one the file is plain text, as before, and a lost password cannot be recovered

- **Send feedback** (Help → Send feedback…): say what it is about and attach only what you choose — the app picks the part of the log that matters — and see exactly what is sent. Ask for an answer and it comes in the app (Help → Answers to feedback), with no e-mail or account; when the answer asks for more, reply to it right there, still with no name attached. After an error or a crash the app offers to send a report, and never interrupts a video for it. - **Nerd** (Settings → Nerd, off by default): settings for those who like to tinker. Turned on, five pages fold out under it in a colour of their own — Network, Boxes and lists, Player, Remote and automation, Diagnostics — and the everyday pages get a link row to them. Every Nerd setting is tried for real and says whether it works (✓, ! or ✕, with Test and Details); with Nerd off, none of it applies
- **Nerd — the network**: a user agent, referer, headers and proxy, for all sources or one; an IP version per source (IPv4 only, IPv6 only, prefer IPv6); **own DNS** over HTTPS (Cloudflare, Quad9, Google or your own), for all sources or one; **a trusted certificate** for one source — shown first, then exactly that certificate pinned for its server, and a question before casting it, since the TV fetches the stream itself; a **VPN guard** that starts nothing while the VPN is down; **backup addresses** for a source, tried in turn when its server cannot be reached or answers with a server error; **Test connection** — DNS, TCP, TLS, the login with the account's end and connections, and a live channel's first byte and speed against SD/HD/Full HD/4K, step by step; and **Other networks (VLANs)**, the other parts of a home network that Find on my network and the cast panel ask address by address

- **Nerd — the player**: audio passthrough, video sync and motion interpolation, the channel start and buffer memory, **low latency for live** (a smaller buffer, a quick probe, HLS from the newest segment — live channels from the provider only), a highest quality for adaptive streams, mpv options, a config folder and shaders, and **a channel's own settings** (audio and subtitle language, deinterlacing, aspect ratio, audio delay)
- **Nerd — remote and automation**: a **remote API** with a token (play a channel by name or number, the channel list, what plays, TV mode and its keys), and every source shared as one M3U and one XMLTV guide for other players in the house; **webhooks** and **MQTT** for events (playing, paused, stopped, channel changed, recordings) with Home Assistant discovery — and, with Take commands on, Home Assistant's buttons, volume, a favourites list, a box to play a channel by name or number and a TV mode switch; recording file names from a template, and a command run when a recording is done. It reads dopeIPTV's own rewind buffer, never a second connection
