  • We do not know who you are, and we do not try to find out. You have no account with us, and what we keep holds no names, no e-mail addresses and no IP addresses. The only things that can be tied to a person are an e-mail you send us yourself and a payment, which Stripe handles.

  • The website's statistics are made anonymous in several steps. No cookies and no outside analytics services. Your IP address is turned at once into a country and a pseudonymous ID and is never written into the statistics, and the logs that contain it are deleted automatically after a week (the front server's operating logs after at most 30 days). After 13 months even the ID goes, and only anonymous figures remain.

  • What we never collect: what you watch, your sources, channels and logins, and where you are more precisely than the country. We use no cookies that follow you, sell no data, show no ads and have no trackers from others – not even the fonts come from anyone else.

1. Who is responsible

Solecraft AB (company registration number 559601-3176), Örebro, Sweden, is the controller for the processing of personal data described here. Contact us at info@solecraft.se – also when you want to use your rights (section 10).

This policy covers the website dopeiptv.org (the old address iptv.dope.rs only forwards there), the services on it – support (tips and monthly support), feedback and error reports, settings sync, watch together and updates – and the dopeIPTV app as we publish it.

2. The dopeIPTV app

What stays on your computer

Everything you add or do in the app – your sources and their logins, favourites, watch history, settings, recordings and cached data – is stored on your own computer. We cannot see it. The app has no analytics or telemetry, no advertising and no account with us.

Private viewing: while it is on, nothing of what you play is kept – no history, no position to resume from and no Trakt scrobble.

When the app contacts dopeiptv.org

  • Updates. Once a day (every six hours if you have chosen test builds) the app asks dopeiptv.org which version is the newest, and an update you install is downloaded through dopeiptv.org. The request names the app and its version, the operating system and the processor type – for example dopeIPTV/1.5.0 (Linux; x86_64) – and, like every request on the internet, comes from your IP address. We count updates as described in section 3. If dopeiptv.org does not answer, the app asks GitHub instead. You can turn the check off in the app's settings (Check for updates on startup).
  • Error reports and feedback are sent only when you press Send. You see the whole report first and can change or delete anything in it. Before you see it, the app has already removed web addresses, IP and MAC addresses, e-mail addresses, logins and access tokens, your user name and home folder, the names, servers and logins of your sources, channel names and the titles you have watched. What reaches us: the kind of report (error, crash or feedback), the app version, the name of the operating system, the text, for feedback the topic and area you chose, whether the app holds a supporter key (only “yes” – never the key), whether the app is an official build or runs from the source code and, if you asked for an answer, a hash of a random code that only your computer knows. We clean the text once more and store the report without an IP address or browser details. For 30 days the app now and then asks whether there is an answer, sending only those hashes.
  • Settings sync (for everyone, only if you turn it on). What you choose to sync – favourites, settings and, if you turn them on, playlists with their logins and watch history – is encrypted on your computer, before anything is sent, with a key made from a sync code that only your computers know. Our server stores only the encrypted data, under a random-looking number that is also made from that code, with a version number and the time it was written. We cannot read it and cannot tell whose it is: sync needs no account, no e-mail address, no name and no supporter key, and nothing that identifies you is sent. Sync requests are not logged. One synced copy can be at most 2 MB and all copies together at most 5 GB; when that space is full, a new copy has to wait, while copies already stored can still be updated. Data that no computer has read or written for 180 days is deleted, and you can delete it yourself in the app (Stop syncing on this computer, then also delete the synced copy).
  • Watch together (only while you are in a room). The app keeps your player in step with your friends' players, each of you playing from your own source. Who is in the room (the name you choose for it), what plays and play, pause and seek go between your computers encrypted with a key made from the room code, which only the people in the room know; our server only passes the encrypted messages on. It sees the room under a name also made from the code, a random number for each person in it, how big the messages are and when they are sent – not who you are, the names or what you watch. Nothing is written to disk: the server keeps a room's latest messages in memory and forgets the room an hour after the last request. Watch together requests are not logged. It cannot be used while private viewing is on.
  • Official builds only. When the app sends an error report or feedback, syncs or watches together, it also says whether it is an official build from us, runs from the source code or has been modified. A modified build may not use those services – a problem in it is reported to whoever made it. We only count how many requests are refused per day and service, without an IP address or anything else that shows who it was.
  • The supporter key is checked inside the app, offline. Nothing is sent to check it.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in delivering updates and knowing how many are installed, and in fixing the problems you report (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR); for settings sync and watch together, providing the service you have asked for (Article 6(1)(b)).

Services the app uses directly

When you use the features below, the app connects from your computer straight to these services. We receive nothing from it, and each service's own terms and privacy policy apply.

  • Your own TV services and sources – the Xtream Codes, M3U, Stalker or other sources and the boxes on your network that you add. The app connects to the addresses you have entered, with the logins you have given, so the service sees your IP address and what you play.
  • TMDB (The Movie Database) – posters, descriptions and the week's popular films and series. The app sends titles (and years), never your logins or the address of your service. On by default; choose Provider artwork under Settings → Posters & info to turn it off.
  • OpenSubtitles.com – when you search for subtitles: the title, the language and, for your own files, a fingerprint (a hash) of the file, as well as your OpenSubtitles login if you have added one.
  • Trakt – if you connect your Trakt account: what you watch, and your watchlist and history. Signing in goes through your browser and dopeiptv.org/trakt/callback/, which passes the one-time sign-in code straight on to the app on your computer without keeping it.
  • Apple (iTunes Search) – missing cover art for your own music files: the artist and album name. Can be turned off (Look up missing album art online).
  • SiliconDust – the programme guide for an HDHomeRun tuner you add, using the tuner's own guide code.
  • Chromecast and smart TVs (DLNA) – found and played to on your local network. For this the app may run a small server on your network.
  • Webhooks, MQTT and the remote API (the Nerd settings) – only to the addresses you set yourself.
  • Demo channels – public test streams from Mux, Apple and Bitmovin. When you play them, their servers see your IP address.
  • GitHub – when dopeiptv.org does not answer an update check, and for downloading test builds.

3. The website

Server logs

To send you a page, our web server needs your IP address, and your browser also sends the address of the page, the time, the name and version of your browser and system (the user agent), the page you came from (the referrer) and the languages you prefer.

  • The front server, which handles the encrypted connection, keeps a general technical log of requests and a log of errors, for operation and security. They are rotated every night and kept for at most 30 days, then removed automatically. Settings sync and watch together requests are never logged.
  • For the statistics below, the server keeps two separate logs with IP address, time, address, status, referrer, browser and language: one of page views and the pages' view signal, and one of download clicks. Both are turned over every night and kept for about a week, then removed automatically. They are the only places where your IP address is written down.
  • The services behind the website – the supporter key, feedback, sync and watch together – keep IP addresses only in memory, briefly – at most a day – to limit how many requests one address can make.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in running the website securely (Article 6(1)(f)).

Statistics

We count visits ourselves: without cookies, without storing anything in your browser and without outside analytics services. The purpose is to understand how people find and use the website and the app, so that we can make them better – not to know who anyone is.

When a page has opened in your browser, it sends a short signal to our server with: the page; the page you came from; the language of the page and of your browser; your screen size; whether the browser says it is automated; how long the page was shown; whether a pointer, touch or keyboard was used (only that it happened); whether a link to tip or support was followed or a payment completed; and some of what you do on the page – help questions you open, words you search for in the help and how many answers were found, parts of the front page you scroll to, installation instructions you open, a change of language, and the address (the site only) of a link to another site that you follow.

How a visit is made anonymous, step by step:

  1. At once. Every fifteen minutes the server reads the log. The IP address is used for two things and is then not written into the statistics: a country, looked up in a country database on our own server (DB-IP's free database – nothing is sent anywhere), and a pseudonymous visitor ID: a shortened one-way hash of the IP address and the browser name, made with a secret key that exists only on our server. The ID does not show the address, we do not link it to anything else and we never try to find out who is behind it – it exists only so that we can count unique and returning visitors.
  2. After about a week the log with the IP addresses is deleted automatically.
  3. After 13 months (400 days) the ID, the time of day, the screen size and the version numbers are removed, the time is rounded to ten seconds and the visit is mixed with the rest of the month, so that their order tells nothing. Only the day, country, browser and system, type of device, page language, how the visitor came, the number of pages and whether there was a download or a support link followed remain. That is no longer personal data and is kept without a time limit, as are the totals per day.
  4. After 25 months the last visitor IDs are deleted; they are kept that long only to compare one year with the year before.

Until step 3 each visit is kept with the ID, country, time, browser and version, operating system, type of device, screen size, languages, how you found us (the linking site and page, without search words or other parameters; a ?ref= tag on our own links), the pages and the time on each, downloads, the support links, the actions above and whether you have been here before.

Visits from non-public addresses and from the website's administrators are not counted, and visits that look as if they come from robots are marked as such.

For updates the app downloads (section 2), we save the time, the file, the version that was updated, the operating system and processor, the country and whether it came from our own network – never the IP address and no ID. They are deleted after 400 days; only totals per day remain.

Only the website's administrators can see the statistics. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in understanding and improving the website and the app (Article 6(1)(f)). We have chosen methods that do not follow you: no cookies, no outside services, pseudonymous IDs and fixed deletion times. You can object at any time (section 10).

The feedback form

The Feedback link at the foot of every page works like feedback from the app: you choose a topic and whether it is about the app or the website, and write your text. E-mail addresses and web addresses in the text are removed, and the feedback is stored without an IP address or browser details. Because we do not know who sent it, we cannot answer – if you want an answer, e-mail us. Your browser remembers when you last sent feedback, so that there is a minute between two.

4. Cookies and storage in your browser

  • The cookie lang is set only when you choose a language in the language menu, and remembers your choice for a year. It is needed for the function you asked for.
  • Local storage (kept in your browser, and read there by our pages): dn-currency – the currency you chose in the tip dialog; dn-wallet – whether your device offered Apple Pay or Google Pay, so that the right button shows at once; dn-supporter-key – your supporter key after a tip or monthly support, so that the tip dialog and the support page can show it again; fbs-last – when you last sent feedback. You can clear them in your browser's settings.
  • Administrators only: the website's administrators have a sign-in cookie and a mark that keeps their own visits out of the statistics. These are never set for other visitors.

We use no cookies for advertising or tracking, and even the fonts come from our own server.

5. Tips and monthly support

Tips and monthly support are paid through Stripe (Stripe Payments Europe, Ltd., 1 Grand Canal Street Lower, Dublin 2, Ireland): a tip in Stripe's payment form in the tip dialog on our pages, monthly support on Stripe's own payment page, which our support page links to. You enter your card details in Stripe's form or on Stripe's page; they go straight to Stripe and never reach us. Stripe is responsible for its own processing of the payment, under its own privacy policy (stripe.com/privacy). For monthly support Stripe keeps your e-mail address and the subscription, so that it can charge each month and send receipts until you cancel. Our pages load Stripe's script and form only when you point at or open a tip, and Stripe's form and page may use their own cookies, for example to prevent fraud.

  • What we see: the amount, currency and time, whether it was a tip or monthly support, how you paid (by card or with a digital wallet such as Apple Pay or Google Pay) and the name or e-mail address you gave for the receipt. Our administrators see this on the website's admin page, and as a notification on the devices where they have turned notifications on. Our server fetches it from Stripe when needed and keeps it only in memory, for a few minutes; it does not save it. The notifications are sent encrypted through the push service of the administrator's browser (Apple, Google, Mozilla or Microsoft), which cannot read them.
  • For a tip our server asks Stripe to start the payment, with the amount and currency you chose. With Apple Pay or Google Pay, the wallet's one-time payment token, the e-mail address for the receipt and the name the wallet passes on go through our server on their way to Stripe without being saved.
  • After paying – in the tip dialog, through the link in a tip's receipt, or when Stripe sends you back to our support page – the page asks our server for your supporter key, and the server asks Stripe whether that payment is paid. Nothing about you is saved on our side.
  • The supporter key contains no personal data (see the payment terms). We note that a key has been handed out – a number made from the payment, the time and how – so that we can count the keys.
  • Bookkeeping: tips and monthly support are part of Solecraft AB's accounts, which the Swedish Bookkeeping Act (bokföringslagen) requires us to keep for seven years.

Legal basis: carrying out the payment you have asked for (Article 6(1)(b)), our legal obligation to keep and preserve accounts (Article 6(1)(c)) and our legitimate interest in knowing about tips and support as they come in (Article 6(1)(f)).

The services the app uses directly (section 2) get their data from your app, not from us.

9. How long we keep data

Most of what we keep says nothing about who you are: no name, no e-mail address, no IP address, no account. Only two things are tied to a person – an e-mail you send us, and a payment (at Stripe and in our accounts). Everything else holds nothing that points to you, or is kept only briefly and then deleted automatically.

  • Server logs – the only places where an IP address is written down, with the browser and what was fetched, nothing else about you: the statistics' two logs about a week, the front server's operating logs at most 30 days. Then removed automatically.
  • Statistics – no IP address and no name, only a pseudonymous ID: each visit for 400 days, then fully anonymous (without ID, time of day or screen size); the last IDs are deleted after 25 months. Update records, never with an IP address or ID: 400 days.
  • Error reports and feedback – no IP address, no name, no e-mail address, and cleaned of anything identifying before they are stored: 12 months, then removed automatically.
  • Synced data – encrypted on your computer, so we cannot read it or tell whose it is: until you delete it, or 180 days after it was last used.
  • Watch together – encrypted, in memory only, never on disk: until an hour after the room's last request.
  • Request limits – the services behind the website keep IP addresses only in memory, for at most a day, to stop abuse. They are never written down.
  • Payments – the name or e-mail address you give for the receipt: at Stripe under its terms, and in our accounts for seven years, as the Swedish Bookkeeping Act requires. Our server itself saves none of it.
  • E-mail you send us: as long as the matter needs.
  • In your browser – on your own device, where we cannot see it: until you clear it (the language cookie for a year).

10. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

  • get access to the personal data we hold about you (a copy);
  • have incorrect data corrected;
  • have data erased;
  • have the processing restricted;
  • object to processing based on our legitimate interest – for example the statistics;
  • receive data you have given us in a machine-readable format (data portability), when the processing is based on an agreement.

In practice we hold almost nothing that tells us who you are. We have no accounts, no names and no e-mail addresses – except in an e-mail you send us, and the name or e-mail address you give Stripe when you pay. The statistics keep no IP addresses, only a pseudonymous ID that does not show the address and that we do not use to find out who you are. Reports and feedback are stored without an IP address, browser details or e-mail address. Synced data and watch together are encrypted, so that we can neither read them nor tell whose they are. On our side your IP address is written down only in the server logs, which are deleted automatically after a week (the front server's operating logs after at most 30 days, section 9).

Write to info@solecraft.se. We answer within a month, free of charge. Because we cannot pick you out ourselves, we usually need your help to find anything at all: for your visits in the statistics, the IP address and the browser you used and roughly when; for a report or a piece of feedback, what you wrote; for a payment, the date, the amount and the e-mail address on the receipt. Without that we often hold nothing we can link to you – and then there is nothing to show, correct or erase.

We make no automated decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects on you.

You can complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten, IMY), imy.se, or to the data protection authority where you live. We would be glad if you contacted us first.

11. Security

The website can only be reached over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Statistics, reports and other data are kept on our server outside the public website, and only the website's administrators can see them. They sign in with a passkey or a password and, if they choose, a one-time code. Card details never reach us, and synced data is encrypted on your computer before it leaves it.

12. Changes

When we change how we process personal data, we update this policy and the date at the top. We also announce larger changes on the website.
