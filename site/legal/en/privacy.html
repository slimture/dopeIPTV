2. The dopeIPTV app

What stays on your computer

Everything you add or do in the app – your sources and their logins, favourites, watch history, settings, recordings and cached data – is stored on your own computer. We cannot see it.

The app has no analytics or telemetry, no advertising and no account with us.

Private viewing: while it is on, nothing of what you play is kept:

  • no history, resume positions, statistics or watched marks;
  • nothing to Trakt, Simkl, Discord or your own media server;
  • and Watch together is off.

When the app contacts dopeiptv.org

Only for what is described below. Audit us shows the code that does each part.

Updates

Once a day (every six hours if you have chosen test builds) the app asks dopeiptv.org which version is the newest, and an update you install is downloaded through dopeiptv.org. If dopeiptv.org does not answer, the app asks GitHub instead.

The request names the app and its version, the operating system and the processor type – for example dopeIPTV/1.5.0 (Linux; x86_64) – and, like every request on the internet, comes from your IP address. We count updates as described in section 3.

You can turn the check off in the app's settings (Check for updates on startup).

Error reports and feedback

They are sent only when you press Send. You see the whole report first and can change or delete anything in it.

Before you see it, the app has already removed:

  • web addresses, IP and MAC addresses and e-mail addresses;
  • logins and access tokens;
  • your user name and home folder;
  • the names, servers and logins of your sources;
  • channel names and the titles you have watched.

What reaches us:

  • the kind of report (error, crash or feedback), the app version and the name of the operating system;
  • the text, which also names the versions of the app, the operating system, Python, Qt and mpv, and how video is drawn;
  • for feedback, the topic, area and how often you chose;
  • whether the app is an official build or runs from the source code;
  • if you asked for an answer, a hash of a random code that only your computer knows.

We clean the text once more and store the report without an IP address or browser details. For 30 days the app now and then asks whether there is an answer, sending only those hashes.

Settings sync

For everyone, only if you turn it on. What you choose to sync – favourites, settings and, if you turn them on, playlists with their logins and watch history – is encrypted on your computer, before anything is sent, with a key made from a sync code that only your computers know.

Our server stores only the encrypted data, under a random-looking number that is also made from that code, with a version number and the time it was written. We cannot read it and cannot tell whose it is: sync needs no account, no e-mail address and no name, and nothing that identifies you is sent. Sync requests are not logged.

The number alone opens nothing: every request also carries a credential made from the same code, of which the server keeps only a one-way hash. Once a computer with dopeIPTV 1.6.9 or later has synced, the server refuses to read, replace or delete the data for anyone who does not have it.

  • Space: one synced copy can be at most 2 MB and all copies together at most 5 GB. When that space is full, a new copy has to wait, while copies already stored can still be updated.
  • Deleting: data that no computer has read or written for 180 days is deleted, and you can delete it yourself in the app (Stop syncing on this computer, then also delete the synced copy).

Watch together

Only while you are in a room. The app keeps your player in step with your friends' players, each of you playing from your own source.

Who is in the room (the name you choose for it), what plays and play, pause and seek go between your computers encrypted with a key made from the room code, which only the people in the room know. Our server only passes the encrypted messages on.

  • What our server sees: the room under a name also made from the code, a random number for each person in it, how big the messages are and when they are sent – not who you are, the names or what you watch.
  • Who it lets in: from dopeIPTV 1.6.11 every request also carries a credential made from the same code, of which the server keeps only a one-way hash, in memory with the room. The first request binds the room to it, and from then on the server turns away anyone without it: knowing the room's name is not enough to put anything in the room, take a place in it or read from it. A room only older versions use is not bound; someone who joins a bound room later with an older version is turned away.
  • What it keeps: nothing is written to disk. The server keeps a room's latest messages in memory and forgets the room an hour after the last request, and a day after it was made at the latest. Watch together requests are not logged.

It cannot be used while private viewing is on.

Official builds only

When the app looks for updates, sends an error report or feedback, syncs or watches together, it also says whether it is an official build from us, runs from the source code or has been modified.

A modified build does not use those services – it looks for no updates here, and a problem in it is reported to whoever made it. We only count how many requests are refused per day and service, without an IP address or anything else that shows who it was.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in delivering updates and knowing how many are installed, and in fixing the problems you report (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR); for settings sync and watch together, providing the service you have asked for (Article 6(1)(b)).

Services the app uses directly

When you use the features below, the app connects from your computer straight to these services. We receive nothing from it, and each service's own terms and privacy policy apply.

  • Your own TV services and sources – the Xtream Codes, M3U, Stalker or other sources and the boxes on your network that you add. The app connects to the addresses you have entered, with the logins you have given, so the service sees your IP address and what you play.
  • TMDB (The Movie Database) – posters, descriptions and the week's popular films and series. The app sends titles (and years), never your logins or the address of your service. On by default; choose Provider artwork, TMDB off under Settings → Posters & info to turn it off.
  • OpenSubtitles.com – when you search for subtitles: the title, the language and, for your own files, a fingerprint (a hash) of the file, as well as your OpenSubtitles login if you have added one.
  • Trakt – if you connect your Trakt account: what you watch, and your watchlist and history. Signing in goes through your browser and dopeiptv.org/trakt/callback/, which passes the one-time sign-in code straight on to the app on your computer without keeping it.
  • Simkl – if you connect your Simkl account: what you watch, your watched marks and your lists. You sign in with a code at simkl.com/pin.
  • Plex (plex.tv) – if you add a Plex server: the sign-in in your browser at app.plex.tv, and the list of your account's servers.
  • radio-browser.info – the station lists while you use Radio; the app names only itself and its version. Which station you play is told only if you turn on Tell radio-browser.info which stations I play, and never during private viewing.
  • YouTube, Twitch and the like (yt-dlp) – when you open such an address, yt-dlp fetches the stream from that site. If you ask the app to, it downloads the official yt-dlp from GitHub and checks it against its published checksums.
  • Discord – if you turn on Discord status: the Discord app on your computer is told what plays (or only “Watching TV”, as you choose), and Discord shows it to your friends. Nothing during private viewing.
  • Apple (iTunes Search) – missing cover art for your own music files: the artist and album name. Can be turned off (Look up missing album art online).
  • SiliconDust – the programme guide for an HDHomeRun tuner you add, using the tuner's own guide code.
  • Chromecast, smart TVs (DLNA), Kodi and, on a Mac, an Apple TV (AirPlay) – found and played to on your local network. For this the app may run a small server on your network while it casts.
  • The phone remote and the server for the home – only when you turn them on: small servers that answer only devices on your home network.
  • Webhooks, MQTT and the remote API (the Nerd settings) – only to the addresses you set yourself.
  • Your own DNS (the Nerd settings) – if you turn it on, names are looked up at Cloudflare, Quad9, Google or a DNS-over-HTTPS server you choose, instead of your system's.
  • Demo channels – public test streams from Mux, Apple and Bitmovin. When you play them, their servers see your IP address.
  • GitHub – when dopeiptv.org does not answer an update check, for downloading test builds, and for yt-dlp when you ask for it.

3. The website

Server logs

To send you a page, our web server needs your IP address, and your browser also sends the address of the page, the time, the name and version of your browser and system (the user agent), the page you came from (the referrer) and the languages you prefer.

  • The front server, which handles the encrypted connection, keeps no log of the requests to dopeiptv.org, and writes an error down only when something stops it working (below). Settings sync and watch together requests are never logged anywhere.
  • Nothing else is logged: the server behind it writes no log of other requests, and none that holds your IP address. For the statistics below it hands each page view, the pages' view signal and each download click straight to our own service over a socket on the same machine – with IP address, time, the page asked for (only the page – never what a link added to its address), status, browser and language –, which counts it in memory within a minute and writes no IP address anywhere (below). For the public download counter it keeps one small log of download clicks: the file's name, the answer and whether a browser, the app or a robot asked – no IP address, no time, no browser. It is turned over every night and only the day before is kept – two days at the most –, then removed automatically.
  • The services behind the website – tips, feedback, sync and watch together – keep IP addresses only in memory, briefly – at most a day – to limit how many requests one address can make. To limit how many watch together rooms one address makes, a room keeps a keyed hash of the address that made it – never the address – in memory, for as long as the room is kept.

Every log on our server, what it holds and how long it is kept:

  • iptv.dope.rs-dl-counts.log – the download counter's log above: no IP address; two days at the most. Only the web server and the job that counts downloads can read it (mode 640).
  • error.log, one on each of our two web servers – only what stops the web server working, which is rare. Such a line names the request it happened on, and on the front server it can name your IP address. They are turned over every night and the six days before are kept – a week at the most –, then deleted automatically. Only the web server can read them (mode 600).
  • iptv-service.log – the services' own log: when they start and how they are set up, a fault by its kind and place, why Stripe refused a payment (in Stripe's codes), a notification that could not be sent, a refused sign-in to the admin page and why, the statistics' own lines (when the country database was fetched, how many lines could not be read – numbers), and once a day how many things came and went (numbers). No IP address, no request, nothing you sent or typed. Five old copies of at most a megabyte each are kept – at its pace months, possibly years. Only the services can read it (mode 600).
  • iptv-sync.log – the job that copies each new release of the app from GitHub and builds the pages (not settings sync): which release and files, how many downloads were counted (a number). Nothing about a visit. Kept like the services' log (mode 600).

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in running the website securely (Article 6(1)(f)).

Statistics

We count visits ourselves: without cookies, without storing anything in your browser and without outside analytics services. The purpose is to understand how people find and use the website and the app, so that we can make them better – not to know who anyone is.

When a page has opened in your browser, it sends a short signal to our server with:

  • the page, and the page you came from;
  • the language of the page and of your browser;
  • your screen size;
  • whether the browser says it is automated;
  • how long the page was shown;
  • whether a pointer, touch or keyboard was used (only that it happened);
  • whether a link to tip or support was followed or a payment completed;
  • some of what you do on the page: help questions you open, words you search for in the help and how many answers were found, parts of the front page you scroll to, installation instructions you open, a change of language, and the address (the site only) of a link to another site that you follow.

How a visit is made anonymous, step by step:

  1. In memory, within a minute. The web server writes no log of it: it hands the signal straight to our service, which counts it within a minute. There the IP address is used for two things and then forgotten – it is never written down:

    • a country, looked up in a country database on our own server (DB-IP's free database – nothing is sent anywhere);
    • a visitor mark: a shortened keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256) of the IP address and the browser name, made with a secret key of the month that is kept only in memory – in the service's own, or in a file on a file system held in memory (tmpfs) so that a restart of the service keeps it – never on a disk, so never in a backup, and is forgotten after two months. Should the system move memory out to swap, our server's swap is encrypted with a key of its own that a restart of the computer forgets; the service checks this and warns us on its admin page if it is not so.

    The mark is not written down either. A visit is kept with a name for its visitor that holds for that day alone – nothing kept ties your visits of two different days together – and with the mark sealed with the month's key, which only the running service can open; without the key it is random. How many different visitors came over a week or a month is kept as fields of bits (sketches), never as a list of marks – and, since a visitor sets the same bits on each day of a month, only in the service's memory beside the key, never on a disk. We never try to find out who is behind a visit.

  2. For 7 days in detail, but coarse: the visit's time to the half hour, never to the second; the browser's main version; the kind of screen (a phone, a laptop and so on), never its size; the page's language and the browser's without its country; the site that linked by the name its owner registered, never a sub-name made for one visitor; a ?ref= tag only when it is one of our own or names a site everyone knows.

  3. After 7 days the day's visits are deleted, every one – no row of a visit is kept after that, not even an anonymous one.

    What remains are the day's counts: how many visits, pages, downloads and support links followed, how long pages were looked at, and the same per way in (search, social, other sites…). Its lists – countries, languages, browser and system versions, linking sites, tags – keep no name that fewer than five visitors had that day: those become “other”, and are never added up with other days under their name, so someone who came five times is never counted as five. These counts are kept without a time limit. On a quiet day even a count can say that one visitor came from a certain country, so we do not call them impossible to trace back – only so coarse that we cannot, and do not try to, tell who was behind them.

  4. When the month after the visit's month has ended the sketches of that month are deleted: only the number of different visitors in the month and in each week of it is kept. The month's key is forgotten then too – it was only ever in memory, and a restart of the computer forgets it at once (a restart of the service alone keeps it when it waits on the file system in memory).

Until step 3 each visit is kept with:

  • a name for its visitor that holds for that day alone and its mark sealed, the country, and the time to the half hour;
  • browser and its main version, operating system, type of device, the kind of screen and the languages (the browser's without its country);
  • how you found us (the linking site by its registered name; a ?ref= tag on our own links) – never the page that linked: its path could name someone;
  • the pages and the time on each, downloads, the support links you opened (never whether you paid: a payment counts only in the day's total) and the actions above;
  • whether you have been here before.

Visits from non-public addresses and from the website's administrators are not counted, and visits that look as if they come from robots are marked as such.

For updates the app downloads (section 2), we save the hour, the file, the version that was updated, the operating system and processor, the country and whether it came from our own network – never the IP address, the exact time or an ID. They are deleted after 30 days; only totals per day remain.

Only the website's administrators can see the statistics.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in understanding and improving the website and the app (Article 6(1)(f)). We have chosen methods that do not follow you: no cookies, no outside services, no IP address written down, keys only in memory, nothing that ties two days of a visitor together, and fixed deletion times. You can object at any time (section 10).

The feedback form

The Feedback link at the foot of every page works like feedback from the app: you choose a topic and whether it is about the app or the website, and write your text. E-mail addresses and web addresses in the text are removed, and the feedback is stored without an IP address or browser details.

5. Tips

Tips are paid through Stripe (Stripe Payments Europe, Ltd., 1 Grand Canal Street Lower, Dublin 2, Ireland), in Stripe's payment form in the tip dialog on our pages.

You enter your card details in Stripe's form; they go straight to Stripe and never reach us. Stripe is responsible for its own processing of the payment, under its own privacy policy (stripe.com/privacy). Monthly support, offered until 22 November 2026, has ended; Stripe keeps its record of such a subscription under its own policy.

Our pages load Stripe's script and form only when you point at or open a tip, and Stripe's form may use its own cookies, for example to prevent fraud.

  • What we see: the amount, currency and time, how you paid (by card or with a digital wallet such as Apple Pay or Google Pay) and the name or e-mail address you gave for the receipt. Our administrators see this on the website's admin page, and as a notification on the devices where they have turned notifications on.
  • Our server fetches it from Stripe when needed and keeps it only in memory, for a few minutes; it does not save it. The notifications are sent encrypted through the push service of the administrator's browser (Apple, Google, Mozilla or Microsoft), which cannot read them.
  • For a tip our server asks Stripe to start the payment, with the amount and currency you chose. With Apple Pay or Google Pay, the wallet's one-time payment token, the e-mail address for the receipt and the name the wallet passes on go through our server on their way to Stripe without being saved.
  • Bookkeeping: tips are part of Solecraft AB's accounts, which the Swedish Bookkeeping Act (bokföringslagen) requires us to keep for seven years.

Legal basis: carrying out the payment you have asked for (Article 6(1)(b)), our legal obligation to keep and preserve accounts (Article 6(1)(c)) and our legitimate interest in knowing about tips as they come in (Article 6(1)(f)).

9. How long we keep data

Most of what we keep says nothing about who you are: no name, no e-mail address, no IP address, no account. Only two things are tied to a person – an e-mail you send us, and a payment (at Stripe and in our accounts). Everything else holds nothing that points to you, or is kept only briefly and then deleted automatically – and what is deleted is gone from our server's backups within two days as well.

  • Server logs – no log keeps an IP address as a rule: the statistics' lines go to our service's memory, and the download counter's log holds the file, the answer and the kind of browser alone, for two days at the most. The front server keeps no log of requests. A web server's error line, which is rare, can name an address; the error logs are kept for a week at the most; the services' own logs hold no IP address and are kept for months (section 3).
  • Statistics – no IP address, no name and no mark of a visitor: each visit for 7 days as a visit of its day alone, then deleted; the sketches that count different visitors, in memory only, until the month after has ended, and the key – in memory only – as long; only counts remain. Update records, never with an IP address, exact time or ID: 30 days.
  • Error reports and feedback – no IP address, no name, no e-mail address, and cleaned of anything identifying before they are stored: 12 months, then removed automatically – with our answers to them and your replies to an answer. An answer we write to a whole problem at once holds only our own words and is kept until we remove it.
  • Synced data – encrypted on your computer, so we cannot read it or tell whose it is: until you delete it, or 180 days after it was last used.
  • Watch together – encrypted, in memory only, never on disk: until an hour after the room's last request, a day at the most.
  • Request limits – the services behind the website keep IP addresses only in memory, for at most a day, to stop abuse. They are never written down.
  • Payments – the name or e-mail address you give for the receipt: at Stripe under its terms, and in our accounts for seven years, as the Swedish Bookkeeping Act requires. Our server itself saves none of it.
  • E-mail you send us: as long as the matter needs.
  • In your browser – on your own device, where we cannot see it: until you clear it (the language cookie for a year).

10. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

  • get access to the personal data we hold about you (a copy);
  • have incorrect data corrected;
  • have data erased;
  • have the processing restricted;
  • object to processing based on our legitimate interest – for example the statistics;
  • receive data you have given us in a machine-readable format (data portability), when the processing is based on an agreement.

In practice we hold almost nothing that tells us who you are.

  • We have no accounts, no names and no e-mail addresses – except in an e-mail you send us, and the name or e-mail address you give Stripe when you pay.
  • The statistics keep no IP addresses, only a pseudonymous ID that does not show the address and that we do not use to find out who you are.
  • Reports and feedback are stored without an IP address, browser details or e-mail address.
  • Synced data and watch together are encrypted, so that we can neither read them nor tell whose they are.
  • On our side your IP address is never written down by the statistics or the download counter – only, rarely, in a web server's error line, which is deleted automatically after a week at the most (section 9).

Write to info@solecraft.se. We answer within a month, free of charge.

Because we cannot pick you out ourselves, we usually need your help to find anything at all:

  • for your visits in the statistics, the IP address and the browser you used and roughly when;
  • for a report or a piece of feedback, what you wrote;
  • for a payment, the date, the amount and the e-mail address on the receipt.

Without that we often hold nothing we can link to you – and then there is nothing to show, correct or erase.

We make no automated decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects on you.

You can complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten, IMY), imy.se, or to the data protection authority where you live. We would be glad if you contacted us first.

11. Security

The website can only be reached over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Statistics, reports and other data are kept on our server outside the public website, and only the website's administrators can see them. They sign in with a passkey or a password and, if they choose, a one-time code. Card details never reach us, and synced data is encrypted on your computer before it leaves it. The website's services run in a part of our server of their own that holds nothing else: a firewall lets in only our front server, and out they may only look up names and make encrypted connections.
